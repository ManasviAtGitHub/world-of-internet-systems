World of Internet Systems

Every big site is a set of hard problems wearing a friendly interface. This series takes the famous designs — the load balancers, the hash rings, the timelines — and doesn't just diagram them: it runs them. Each episode is a sequence of live experiments where the naive design visibly collapses, one switch applies the real fix, and the sliders are yours.

The thirty episodes

EP 01 · Internet plumbing The Load Balancer Watch one server drown at 95% utilization, round robin betrayed by a secretly slow machine, a stale-data stampede — and the absurdly good fix of picking the less loaded of two random servers. EP 02 · Internet plumbing The Hash Ring Kill the same cache server in two worlds: hash-mod-N scrambles 83% of keys and stampedes the database; the ring hands over 17% — the provable minimum. Then watch virtual nodes grind the lumpy ring flat. EP 03 · The flagships The Timeline — Twitter's fan-out Sixty reads for every write decide push over pull — until a 31-million-follower tweet clogs the fanout queue for everyone. The hybrid fix, and why the celebrity line is a latency promise, not an efficiency optimum. EP 04 · Internet plumbing The Log — Kafka The same viral spike hits a direct pipeline and a log: one loses a thousand messages, the other converts failure into a lag chart that burns down. Then kill the consumer, rewind history, and watch N×M collapse to N+M. EP 05 · Internet plumbing The Name — DNS The resolution treasure hunt, live. Turn caching off and watch the root melt; move a site and watch stale answers outlive the switch; then replay the 2016 Dyn attack and watch sites die in TTL order. EP 06 · The flagships Press Play — Netflix A CDN made of free boxes inside your ISP: remove it and watch the backbone drown; watch the appliance do its shopping at 3 AM before the premiere; then be the Chaos Monkey and learn why instance death is boring. EP 07 · The flagships The Message — WhatsApp Store-and-forward vs flaky phones, the C10K wall falling to two million connections on one box, and group encryption that uploads once instead of 256 times — the fifty-engineer switchboard, proven. EP 08 · Internet plumbing The Border — BGP Real path-vector routing on a live AS graph: cut the cables, run the 2008 YouTube hijack duel prefix by prefix, then erase Facebook from the internet the way Facebook did — and watch RPKI stop the lie at the border. EP 09 · The flagships The Match — Uber Hexagons vs scanning the whole city, the two-second pause that shortens every ride, and surge pricing as a thermostat — a live marketplace on the H3 grid, priced-out riders counted honestly. EP 10 · The flagships The Index — Google Search A real inverted index (×1,000 speedup, byte-identical answers), a real random surfer converging on PageRank while the keyword stuffer sinks, and the tail at scale — 63% of fan-out queries hit by 1% hiccups, cured by hedging. EP 11 · The flagships The Quorum — Amazon's Dynamo Turn the R+W dial and watch staleness hit exactly zero when the inequality holds; kill two replicas and let hinted handoff keep the cart alive; then split the cart and see why vector clocks beat timestamps. EP 12 · The flagships The Upload — YouTube 500 hours arrive every minute: chunk the tape and go live in 5 minutes instead of 60; relive the thumbnail meltdown that made BigTable necessary; then watch adaptive bitrate ride out dips a fixed player can't. EP 13 · The flagships The Feed — Instagram The famous 64-bit ID, minted and decoded live: kill the ID service nobody needed, verify sort-by-ID equals sort-by-time, and watch an ID cursor read a flat 20 rows while OFFSET pagination walks thousands. EP 14 · Internet plumbing The Page — URL to pixels The famous interview question with a scrubber in your hand: the seven-stage journey, the war on round trips (1.35s of handshakes on satellite), HTTP/2's head-of-line freeze vs QUIC's shrug, and the blocking script. EP 15 · Internet plumbing The Edge — CDN & anycast One IP announced from every city; the cache funnel that lets an origin doze at 0.4 req/s; a 31.4 Tbps storm absorbed at 6% headroom; and the 2019 regex bomb — real engines measured, deployed two different ways. EP 16 · Internet plumbing The Ladder — databases at scale The climb every company makes, and its three tolls: the vanishing comment (replication lag), the acked write that evaporated (async vs sync), and a genuinely implemented Raft election — kill the leader, split the network, and watch majorities refuse to split the brain. EP 17 · The wider world The Guild — Discord A community is literally one Elixir process — run the mailroom into its quadratic wall and un-jam it with Manifold; shield a hot channel with request coalescing (turn the raid up, the database doesn't notice); and watch Go's garbage collector quake every two minutes next to Rust's flat line. EP 18 · The wider world The Workspace — Slack One message's world tour in ~500ms, persist-first; the boot payload that grew into a truck (and the edge cache that put it on a diet); a reconnect storm absorbed at the edge; and the day the job queue wedged because the reservoir was also the turbine — until Kafka turned failure into lag. EP 19 · The wider world The Playlist — Spotify The P2P era rebuilt live: the 265ms illusion (server starts, swarm finishes — the slider can't touch it), one byte in eleven paid for; the event river with bulkheads so a debug surge can't delay the stream that pays artists; and a Discover Weekly computed in front of you — turn off the audio model and this week's releases vanish from your 30. EP 20 · The wider world The Meeting — Zoom Servers that refuse to watch your video: the MCU melts at ~97 participants while the router forwards 15× more; simulcast detaches your downlink from the meeting size; and the 30× cliff of March 2020 replayed day by day — colo at 50%, cloud as the airbag, provisioning dial in your hands. EP 21 · The wider world The Folder — Dropbox A file is a list of hashes: whole-file sync falls hours behind on the same uplink where block sync naps; a teammate's copy costs zero bytes; LAN sync turns the office into the CDN; and Magic Pocket's two durability regimes — replicate fresh, erasure-code cold — race to repair a dead machine. EP 22 · The wider world The Calendar — Airbnb Two guests race for the same nights — widen the checkout window and watch doubles multiply until one transaction locks them out (iCal sync stays racy forever); the Monorail split by the 1% comparison ratchet where users never meet a bug; and a two-sided ranker that books more trips by asking whether the host will say yes. EP 23 · The wider world The Charge — Stripe Exactly once over an at-least-once wire: identical failures hit a blind retrier and an idempotency key — one stacks double charges, the other is structurally zero; a four-layer bouncer sheds test traffic before it ever touches a charge; and the version time machine that walks 2011's API out of 2026's, gate by dated gate. EP 24 · The wider world The Drop — Shopify A celebrity drop vs the same hardware cut two ways: one shared database breaks every shop, twelve pods contain the fire to one cell; the Storefront Renderer's 45ms p75 emerges from its cache mix; and the microservices tax charged honestly — per-hop latency and failure, compounded. EP 25 · The wider world The Onsale — Ticketmaster 14 million people, 2 million seats: the hold with a countdown (a TTL dial that hurts on both ends), the waiting-room lottery that strips bots of their only advantage, and a wall of caches protecting a 1970s VAX engine that must never sell a seat twice. EP 26 · The wider world The Front Page — Reddit The open-sourced hot formula running live (ten times the votes buys exactly one ladder rung); precompute-everything vs sort-on-read at 100 views per vote; Wilson confidence beating raw score against old giant comments; and r/place rebuilt as one 4-bit-per-pixel bitfield. EP 27 · The wider world The Board — Pinterest The 64-bit ID that routes itself (Instagram's sorted; this one addresses); a home feed that's a scored pool, not a timeline — chronology's buried treasures itemized; real Pixie random walks vs the popularity chart, restart dial in hand; and the join that can't shard vs a hundred key lookups through the cache wall. EP 28 · The wider world The Graph — LinkedIn Fan-out on read, done seriously: the slowest of twelve partitions sets your feed's pace; push melts when every employer is a celebrity; early scoring at the leaves moves kilobytes instead of megabytes; and yesterday's Hadoop build ships by atomic pointer flip — zero mixed reads, ever. EP 29 · The wider world The Swipe — Tinder The world cut to equal people (a measured U-curve decides the cell size); two right swipes eighty milliseconds apart — the silent heartbreak of check-then-insert, cured by a pair-keyed stream; and two-second polling priced against a five-byte nudge. EP 30 · The Finale The Encyclopedia — Wikipedia The site that shouldn't be possible: 296 billion pageviews on $3.4M of hosting. The read:write funnel; purge-don't-expire (the fix must vanish NOW — run the vandalism incident in both worlds); replicate the hits, partition the tail; and one template edit dirtying two million pages, paid for lazily. Plus the closing of the whole series.

The rules of the series

The simulations are honest. Queues, latencies, and collapses emerge from the same arithmetic that governs real datacenters — Poisson arrivals, service rates, backlog — never from a script. When measured dots land on a theory curve, it's because the simulation independently agrees with the math.

Every number is sourced. The scale figures quoted in episodes come from engineering blogs, published papers, and conference talks, each cited at the foot of the episode. Behind the site sits a research base covering 29 systems.

Every episode is verified before it ships. Each simulation's exact code is run headlessly against behavioral assertions — the collapse must collapse, the fix must fix — before publishing.
